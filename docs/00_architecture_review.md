**Review type:** Formal pre-development architecture gate. This document does not modify or regenerate the reviewed documents — it is the review board's independent assessment, to be used to refine `01`–`04` before Phase 5 begins.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

**Verdict: NOT YET APPROVED.** This is a substantively strong first-pass architecture — well above the bar typically seen at this stage — but it has 4 Critical and 9 High-priority gaps that a Shopify- or Amazon-caliber review would block on, concentrated in four places: a missing individual-designer role that would prevent the platform's #1 business priority from actually functioning with real staff accounts, an inventory-reservation design that has not been specified as concurrency-safe (real overselling risk), an admin/staff authentication design with no MFA, and a compliance gap around consent tracking for WhatsApp/SMS marketing under India's DPDP Act. None of these require re-architecting anything — they are additions and clarifications to what's already there, not contradictions of it. Section 14 is the complete list of what must be resolved before Phase 5 development starts.

The review board also wants to be explicit that this is not a rubber-stamp exercise softened by praise: the findings below are real, and several (D1, S1, B1) are the kind of gap that would cause a genuine production incident or a broken core workflow if shipped as-is. Section 13 records what the board considers genuinely well done, separately from the gate-blocking findings, so the praise doesn't get lost in — or mistaken for — the critique.

---

## 2. Review Methodology

Each of the four documents was read in full. Findings below were generated by cross-checking every claim against (a) the other three documents for internal consistency, (b) standard production-system practice for a MERN + MongoDB stack at this scale, and (c) the stated business priority order (Interior Design → Lead Generation → Furniture eCommerce) from `01_business_research.md`. Every issue is tagged with the board member(s) who raised it and follows the requested format: **Issue → Why it's a problem → Recommended solution → Priority.**

Priority definitions used throughout:

| Priority | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Critical** | Blocks correct operation of a core workflow, or creates a legal/compliance/security exposure. Must be resolved before Phase 5 starts. |
| **High** | Real production risk (data integrity, security, or a business-priority workflow gap) that should be resolved or explicitly scheduled before Phase 5 starts. |
| **Medium** | Should be resolved during Phase 5 (MVP build), not before it — but must be tracked, not silently dropped. |
| **Low** | Worth fixing, does not block MVP development or launch. |

## 3. Findings — Business Analysis

### B1. No individual "Designer" role exists — only "Design Manager"
**Raised by:** Engineering Manager, Principal Architect
**Issue:** `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §14 and `03_database_design.md` §9.1.2 define RBAC roles as `SUPER_ADMIN`, `SALES_MANAGER`, `DESIGN_MANAGER`, `CATALOG_MANAGER`, `SUPPORT_AGENT`, `CUSTOMER`. There is no `DESIGNER` role. But `design_projects.assignedDesignerId` (`03_database_design.md` §9.4.1) and the Interior Design Workflow (`02_enterprise_architecture.md` §13) clearly assume individual designers are assigned to and work projects.
**Why it is a problem:** Interior Design is the platform's stated #1 business priority. Without a `DESIGNER` role, there are only two options as built: give every designer full `Design Manager` permissions (every designer can see and edit every other designer's projects, quotations, and financials — a real least-privilege violation), or give designers no system access at all and route everything through the Design Manager (defeats the purpose of the workflow entirely). Either way, the core priority-#1 workflow cannot be staffed correctly as currently specified.
**Recommended solution:** Add a `DESIGNER` role scoped to: read/write only on `design_projects` where `assignedDesignerId == self`, read on assigned `leads`/`consultations`/`site_visits`, write on `design_project_assets` (upload site photos/renders) and `stageHistory` notes, no access to other designers' projects or to financial/quotation-approval actions beyond what's explicitly delegated. Update the RBAC table in `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §14 and the `roles` seed data in `03_database_design.md` §9.1.2.
**Priority:** **Critical**

### B2. No returns/RMA workflow or collection for furniture reverse logistics
**Raised by:** Engineering Manager
**Issue:** `01_business_research.md` §3.3 explicitly flags that furniture returns are operationally different from apparel returns (pickup scheduling, damage inspection, restocking-fee logic) and calls this out as a critical gap to fix. `02_enterprise_architecture.md` and `03_database_design.md` never follow through: `orders.paymentStatus` has a `REFUNDED` value, but there is no `returns`/`rma` collection, no return-pickup scheduling, no condition-inspection or partial-refund workflow anywhere in the schema or the Order Flow diagram.
**Why it is a problem:** A gap doc01 correctly identified as critical for a *furniture* business (not a generic eCommerce concern) was not carried through into the technical design. As it stands, a customer return has nowhere to be tracked between "customer requests return" and "refund issued."
**Recommended solution:** Add a `returns` collection (`orderId`, `items[]`, `reason`, `status`: `REQUESTED → PICKUP_SCHEDULED → INSPECTED → APPROVED/REJECTED → REFUNDED`, `inspectionNotes`, `restockingFee`, `refundAmount`) and a corresponding Return Flow diagram in `02_enterprise_architecture.md`, reusing the `appointments` collection (already modeled in `03_database_design.md` §9.4.5) for the pickup scheduling step.
**Priority:** **High**

### B3. Delivery/installation scheduling is modeled in the data layer but never wired into the Order Flow
**Raised by:** Staff Engineer, Engineering Manager
**Issue:** `03_database_design.md` §9.4.5 explicitly includes `INSTALLATION` as an `appointmentType` on the `appointments` collection — a direct, correct response to `01_business_research.md`'s "White-glove delivery & assembly scheduling" gap. But the Order Flow sequence diagram in `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §11 goes straight from `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY` to `DELIVERED` with no step that creates an installation appointment.
**Why it is a problem:** This is a fix that was applied in one document (the schema) and never carried through to the process that would actually use it (the flow). As specified today, the `appointments` collection's `INSTALLATION` type has no code path that ever creates a record of that type — it's dead schema.
**Recommended solution:** Add a step to the Order Flow: for orders flagged as requiring installation (large/assembled furniture, a property of the product or category), create an `appointments` record with `appointmentType: INSTALLATION` when the order reaches `PACKED`/`SHIPPED`, and gate `DELIVERED` on the installation appointment reaching `COMPLETED` where applicable.
**Priority:** **High**

### B4. No consent/opt-in tracking for WhatsApp/SMS marketing communications
**Raised by:** Security Architect, Engineering Manager
**Issue:** `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §10 (Lead Generation Flow) sends WhatsApp/SMS/email notifications and nurture sequences as a core mechanism. Neither `leads` nor `newsletter_subscribers` (`03_database_design.md` §9.5.1, §9.6.4) has a `marketingConsent` / `consentTimestamp` / `consentSource` field.
**Why it is a problem:** India's DPDP Act 2023 and TRAI's commercial-communication consent framework require documented, timestamped opt-in for marketing SMS/WhatsApp messages, not just "we have their phone number because they filled out a form." This is a genuine legal exposure for a platform whose #2 business priority is lead generation built substantially on WhatsApp/SMS outreach — not a hypothetical or nice-to-have.
**Recommended solution:** Add `marketingConsent: {granted: Boolean, grantedAt: Date, source: String, channel: [SMS, WHATSAPP, EMAIL]}` to `leads` and `newsletter_subscribers`, gate the nurture-sequence worker (`02_enterprise_architecture.md` §10) on consent per channel, and get explicit legal sign-off on the consent-capture UX before Phase 5 (this was already flagged generically in `01_business_research.md` §9's regulatory risk row, but never turned into a schema/flow requirement — that follow-through is what's missing).
**Priority:** **Critical**

### B5. No vendor/procurement model for the design-project "Procurement" stage
**Raised by:** Solution Architect
**Issue:** The Interior Design state machine (`02_enterprise_architecture.md` §13) includes a `Procurement` stage, but no `vendors` or `purchase_orders` collection exists anywhere in `03_database_design.md` to track what's being procured, from whom, at what cost.
**Why it is a problem:** Procurement cost is a direct input to project margin — without tracking it, there's no way to reconcile a project's actual cost against its quoted BOQ, which undermines the "design projects as B2B-style pipeline objects with real margin visibility" recommendation `01_business_research.md` §10 explicitly calls for.
**Recommended solution:** Defer building this to Phase 1 (consistent with `01_business_research.md`'s phasing), but add a `vendors` collection and a lightweight `procurement_orders` collection to the Phase 1 database design now, so the Procurement stage isn't a black box between Approved and Execution.
**Priority:** **Medium**

### B6. No credit-terms/invoice-then-pay workflow for B2B/bulk buyers
**Raised by:** Solution Architect
**Issue:** The entire `payments` model (`03_database_design.md` §9.3.6) assumes real-time capture via Razorpay. `bulk_enquiries` (§9.5.5) has no linkage to a payment-terms concept (net-30, purchase-order-backed invoicing) common in B2B/commercial furniture and interior contracts.
**Why it is a problem:** If B2B/commercial (flagged as a Phase 1/2 revenue opportunity in `01_business_research.md` §7) is pursued before this is addressed, the payment model will not fit how commercial buyers actually pay.
**Recommended solution:** Not an MVP blocker (B2B is explicitly Phase 1/2). Flag it now as an open item in `01_business_research.md`'s Appendix so it isn't rediscovered mid-Phase-1 as a surprise.
**Priority:** **Medium**

### B7. No architect/contractor referral partner program identified as a revenue and lead channel
**Raised by:** Engineering Manager
**Issue:** `01_business_research.md` §7's revenue opportunities list is thorough but doesn't include a referral-commission program specifically for architects and contractors (distinct from the Designer Partner Network, which is about labor, not referrals) — a channel both Pepperfry and Urban Ladder run.
**Why it is a problem:** This is a low-cost, high-fit lead-generation channel for the platform's #2 priority that was likely missed simply because the competitive research focused on design-labor partnerships, not referral partnerships.
**Recommended solution:** Add as a revenue opportunity in `01_business_research.md` §7, evaluate for Phase 1/2.
**Priority:** **Low**

## 4. Findings — Architecture

### A1. No edge-level rate limiting or bot/DDoS protection ahead of the application-level limiter
**Raised by:** DevOps Architect, Security Architect
**Issue:** `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §16 specifies Redis-backed rate limiting inside the Express application. There is no WAF/edge-level rate limiting or DDoS mitigation (e.g., Cloudflare, AWS WAF/Shield) in front of Nginx anywhere in the deployment diagram (§8).
**Why it is a problem:** Application-level rate limiting only helps after a request has already consumed Nginx and Node resources. A volumetric attack or bot flood against public lead-capture and catalog-search endpoints (both unauthenticated, both business-critical) would exhaust compute before the Express-level limiter ever gets a chance to act.
**Recommended solution:** Add an edge WAF/CDN layer with rate limiting and basic bot-fingerprinting in front of Nginx to the deployment diagram, and add it as a production-readiness checklist item in §17.
**Priority:** **High**

### A2. No CAPTCHA/bot-mitigation on public lead-capture forms
**Raised by:** Staff Engineer, Security Architect
**Issue:** Contact Form, Quote Request, Consultation Booking, and Bulk Enquiry endpoints (`02_enterprise_architecture.md` §10, `03_database_design.md` §9.5) have no bot-mitigation step (reCAPTCHA/hCaptcha/Turnstile) before a submission is scored and inserted.
**Why it is a problem:** Lead Generation is the #2 business priority. Spam/bot submissions would directly corrupt the lead-scoring pipeline and sales-dashboard prioritization — this is not a generic "nice to have," it's a direct threat to the thing the architecture is specifically built to optimize.
**Recommended solution:** Add invisible CAPTCHA verification as a validation step before `LeadService.createLead()` in the Lead Generation Flow (§10), with the score for CAPTCHA-failed-but-retried submissions flagged for manual review rather than silently dropped (to avoid losing genuine leads on false positives).
**Priority:** **High**

### A3. No Transactional Outbox pattern — domain events can be silently lost
**Raised by:** Principal Architect, Staff Engineer
**Issue:** The Lead Generation Flow (`02_enterprise_architecture.md` §10) shows `SVC->>SVC: publish domain event LeadCreated` followed by a separate `SVC->>Q: enqueue notify-sales + notify-customer jobs` step. Nothing in §5–§7 states these are executed within the same transaction/outbox as the `leads` document insert.
**Why it is a problem:** If the process crashes between the DB write and the queue enqueue (or between the event publish and the listener completing), the notification job is lost with no record it should have existed — a lead gets created but sales is never notified and the customer never gets a confirmation. The document is rightly rigorous about idempotency for Razorpay webhooks (§11) but doesn't apply the same rigor to its own internal event-to-queue handoff.
**Recommended solution:** Adopt a Transactional Outbox pattern: write the event to an `outbox` collection in the same logical operation as the domain write (or use MongoDB multi-document transactions per finding D2), with a separate relay process that reads unprocessed outbox entries and enqueues them, guaranteeing at-least-once delivery instead of best-effort.
**Priority:** **High**

### A4. Admin/Analytics module's cross-module read pattern is unspecified
**Raised by:** Principal Architect
**Issue:** The Module Diagram (`02_enterprise_architecture.md` §6) shows `Admin --> Leads`, `Admin --> DesignProjects`, `Admin --> Catalog`, `Admin --> Orders`, `Admin --> Users`, and similarly for `Analytics`. The stated module-boundary rule is that cross-module interaction happens only through an explicitly exported Application-layer service interface or a domain event — but it's never specified whether Admin/Analytics dashboard reads go through each module's Application service (adding request-time coupling and N calls per dashboard load) or through some other read-model mechanism.
**Why it is a problem:** This is exactly the kind of ambiguity that becomes a boundary violation in practice — without an explicit answer, whoever implements the Admin dashboard first will make an ad hoc choice (most likely: reach directly into other modules' repositories "just this once"), and that becomes the unenforced precedent for everyone after.
**Recommended solution:** State explicitly in §6/§7: Admin/Analytics reads go through each owning module's exported read-service interface (not repository), and performance-sensitive aggregate views use dedicated read-models/materialized collections owned by the `analytics` module itself (which also sets up the Phase 2 extraction path already planned in `04_architecture_decision.md` §9.2).
**Priority:** **Medium**

### A5. No graceful shutdown / connection draining specified
**Raised by:** DevOps Architect
**Issue:** `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §17's production-readiness checklist covers health/readiness probes and autoscaling but not SIGTERM handling, in-flight request draining, or DB/queue connection cleanup on container termination.
**Why it is a problem:** Without graceful shutdown, every rolling deploy or autoscale-down event risks dropping in-flight checkout/payment requests mid-transaction — a correctness and customer-trust issue, not just an ops nicety.
**Recommended solution:** Add explicit graceful-shutdown handling (drain in-flight requests, stop accepting new ones, close DB/Redis/BullMQ connections cleanly on SIGTERM with a bounded timeout) as a named checklist item in §17.
**Priority:** **Medium**

### A6. No API contract-testing/OpenAPI strategy
**Raised by:** Staff Engineer
**Issue:** `04_architecture_decision.md` §10.9 places hand-maintained TypeScript contract types in `shared/types/`. There's no OpenAPI spec generation or contract test suite mentioned to keep the actual Express route behavior in sync with those hand-maintained types.
**Why it is a problem:** Hand-maintained shared types drift from real API behavior over time (a classic monorepo failure mode) unless something automatically verifies the contract — the Monorepo's main claimed benefit (Section 6 of `04_architecture_decision.md`) partially depends on this not happening.
**Recommended solution:** Generate an OpenAPI spec from route/validator definitions (or hand-author one) and add a contract-test CI gate; track in `docs/api/` as already planned in `04_architecture_decision.md` §10.7.
**Priority:** **Medium**

### A7. No pre-aggregated/materialized reporting strategy for the Admin Dashboard before Analytics extraction
**Raised by:** Database Architect, DevOps Architect
**Issue:** `03_database_design.md` §11 describes solid on-demand aggregation pipelines, and `04_architecture_decision.md` §9.2 correctly plans to extract Analytics later — but there's no interim strategy (e.g., scheduled daily rollup collections) for keeping the Admin Dashboard fast *while it still lives in the Modular Monolith*, which could be a meaningful window of time.
**Why it is a problem:** Admin Dashboard is one of the six domains explicitly in scope for this review; if its reports are slow even at moderate data volume (well before the Analytics-extraction trigger in `04_architecture_decision.md` §9.2 is met), that's a real UX problem in the interim, not just a someday-problem.
**Recommended solution:** Add scheduled rollup collections (daily/hourly pre-aggregated summaries for the highest-traffic dashboard views) as a Phase 1 item, distinct from and prior to the full Analytics-service extraction.
**Priority:** **Medium**

## 5. Findings — Database

### D1. Inventory reservation is not specified as an atomic conditional update
**Raised by:** Database Architect, Principal Architect
**Issue:** The Order Flow (`02_enterprise_architecture.md` §11) shows `OS->>INV: reserveStock(items)` with no detail on implementation. `03_database_design.md` §9.2.6 describes `quantityAvailable` as denormalized and recomputed "on every write," but never states that reservation itself must be a single atomic `findOneAndUpdate`/`updateOne` with a filter condition (`quantityAvailable >= requestedQty`), rather than a read-then-write.
**Why it is a problem:** If reservation is implemented as "read `quantityAvailable`, check it's sufficient, then write the reservation" (the natural way to write it if not told otherwise), two concurrent checkouts for the last unit of a variant can both pass the check and both reserve — real overselling of physical furniture, which is a direct customer-trust and operational-cost problem, not a theoretical edge case at any meaningful traffic level.
**Recommended solution:** Explicitly document that stock reservation is implemented as a single atomic MongoDB update: `updateOne({_id, quantityAvailable: {$gte: requestedQty}}, {$inc: {quantityReserved: requestedQty, quantityAvailable: -requestedQty}})`, treating a zero-matched-document result as "insufficient stock." Add this to `03_database_design.md` §9.2.6 and reference it explicitly in the Order Flow design notes in `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §11.
**Priority:** **Critical**

### D2. No use of MongoDB multi-document transactions for cross-collection atomic operations
**Raised by:** Database Architect
**Issue:** Several operations span multiple collections and need atomicity — order creation + inventory reservation, lead conversion (update `leads.status` + create `design_projects` + create/update `customers`) — but `03_database_design.md` never mentions MongoDB's native multi-document ACID transactions (available and appropriate given the mandated replica-set deployment, §14.1), relying instead on implicit sequencing.
**Why it is a problem:** Without transactions (or an explicit, documented compensating-action/saga pattern as a deliberate alternative), a crash mid-sequence leaves the system in a partially-applied state — e.g., inventory reserved but no order document, or a lead marked `CONVERTED` with no `design_projects` document actually created.
**Recommended solution:** Explicitly adopt `session.withTransaction()` for the specific, small set of operations that genuinely need cross-collection atomicity (order creation + stock reservation; lead conversion), and document this choice in `03_database_design.md` alongside a rationale for why the *rest* of the system deliberately doesn't use transactions (most cross-module effects are event-driven and can tolerate eventual consistency, per the Hybrid-readiness design in `04_architecture_decision.md`).
**Priority:** **High**

### D3. Audit log snapshots have no PII/sensitive-field redaction
**Raised by:** Database Architect, Security Architect
**Issue:** `audit_logs.before`/`after` (`03_database_design.md` §9.8.2) are described as full document snapshots, and `audit_logs` is explicitly immutable/never-deleted (§3.1). A `users` document update snapshot would include `passwordHash` and full address history with no redaction step mentioned.
**Why it is a problem:** Combined with the "never deleted" rule, this means a password hash (even if bcrypt-hashed, still sensitive) could persist in audit history indefinitely, including hashes that predate a password reset — a real compliance and security-hygiene problem, and in tension with the right-to-erasure posture `03_database_design.md` §15 otherwise correctly tries to establish.
**Recommended solution:** Define an explicit field-redaction/allowlist for what gets captured in `before`/`after` snapshots per entity type — never capture `passwordHash`, token hashes, or full payment card references (not applicable here per the PCI note in §16, but the principle holds); redact to `[REDACTED]` rather than omitting the field silently (so the audit record still shows *that* the field changed).
**Priority:** **High**

### D4. Polymorphic `payments.payableId` query complexity is a real trade-off, undocumented
**Raised by:** Database Architect
**Issue:** `03_database_design.md` §9.3.6 explains *why* `payments` is polymorphic (`payableType: ORDER | DESIGN_PROJECT`) but never notes that this makes any `$lookup`-based join back to the parent (e.g., "show me the order/project name for this payment" in a reporting pipeline) require conditional (`$switch`/dual-`$lookup`) aggregation logic, which is materially more complex than a normal single-target join.
**Why it is a problem:** Not a defect, but an undocumented cost — whoever writes the payments-reporting aggregation later will hit this without warning, and it should be a known, accepted trade-off rather than a surprise.
**Recommended solution:** Add a short "query cost" note alongside the existing rationale in §9.3.6, plus a documented pattern (conditional `$lookup` via `$switch` on `payableType`, or splitting the report into two aggregations unioned via `$unionWith`) for the reporting aggregations that will need it (extending §11.2's pattern).
**Priority:** **Low**

### D5. No milestone-type classification for design-project payments
**Raised by:** Database Architect, Engineering Manager
**Issue:** `design_projects.milestones[]` (`03_database_design.md` §9.4.1) has a free-text `name` field but no structured `milestoneType` (e.g., `DESIGN_FEE`, `MATERIALS`, `LABOR`, `FINAL`) distinguishing revenue categories.
**Why it is a problem:** Design fee, materials cost, and execution labor have different margin profiles and should be reportable separately (this is exactly the kind of unit-economics distinction `01_business_research.md` §5 calls for in its "BI/analytics warehouse separating design-project funnel metrics... with a blended LTV view" recommendation) — free-text milestone names can't be reliably aggregated for that.
**Recommended solution:** Add a `milestoneType` enum field to the milestone sub-document.
**Priority:** **Medium**

### D6. No archival policy for `design_project_assets`
**Raised by:** Database Architect
**Issue:** `03_database_design.md` §14.5 defines archival for `orders`/`design_projects`/`audit_logs`/`lead_activities`/`notifications`, but not for `design_project_assets` — the collection explicitly flagged in §9.4.2 as high-volume and unbounded (the exact reason it's referenced rather than embedded).
**Why it is a problem:** The collection most likely to grow unbounded over time is the one with no lifecycle/archival plan.
**Recommended solution:** Add `design_project_assets` to the archival table (§14.5) with a policy tied to Cloudinary's own storage-tiering options for assets belonging to `HANDOVER`/`LOST`-stage projects older than a defined window.
**Priority:** **Medium**

## 6. Findings — Security

### S1. No MFA/2FA requirement for admin/staff accounts
**Raised by:** Security Architect, Principal Architect
**Issue:** `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §9.1 specifies password + JWT for all authentication, with no distinction for staff/admin accounts. §8.1 mentions the admin app having "stricter network policy (internal/VPN-gated **optional**)" — explicitly optional, not required.
**Why it is a problem:** `SuperAdmin` and other staff roles have access to customer PII, payment references, and business-critical financial data (quotations, milestones, revenue reports). Password-plus-JWT alone, with no second factor, is below the bar any Amazon or Shopify security review would accept for accounts with this level of access — this is not a scale concern, it's a baseline-hygiene one.
**Recommended solution:** Require MFA (TOTP at minimum) for all `STAFF`/`ADMIN` user types before first admin-panel access; make VPN/IP-allowlisting for the admin app a firm requirement, not optional, for production.
**Priority:** **Critical**

### S2. Ambiguity between fine-grained permission-key RBAC and coarse module-level RBAC
**Raised by:** Security Architect, Principal Architect
**Issue:** `03_database_design.md` §9.1.3 designs a proper granular `permissions` collection (`leads.read`, `orders.refund`, etc.) referenced by `roles.permissionIds`. But `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §9's Authentication Flow sequence diagram just says `rbacMiddleware checks role/permission claim` generically, and §14's RBAC table describes access at whole-module granularity ("Sales/Lead Manager: Leads module, read-only Design Projects and Orders"). It's never resolved whether real enforcement is per-permission-key or per-module-role.
**Why it is a problem:** These two documents describe two different security postures. Coarse module-level RBAC is meaningfully more permissive (e.g., "read-only Design Projects" — does that include quotation amounts and customer contact details, or not?) than the fine-grained model doc03 clearly intended to support. Whichever gets implemented by default should be a deliberate choice, not an accident of which document the implementing engineer read more carefully.
**Recommended solution:** Reconcile explicitly: enforce at the permission-key level (matching doc03's design, the more secure and more precise option), with the module-level table in doc02 §14 reframed as a human-readable *summary* of which permission keys each role holds, not the enforcement mechanism itself.
**Priority:** **High**

### S3. No explicit NoSQL-injection mitigation statement
**Raised by:** Security Architect
**Issue:** `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §16 covers Zod validation at the presentation boundary but never explicitly states that schemas must reject non-primitive types where primitives are expected (e.g., rejecting `{"email": {"$ne": null}}` as a login payload) — a well-known MongoDB/Express/Mongoose vulnerability class.
**Why it is a problem:** This is a specific, well-documented, real vulnerability class for exactly this stack (Express + Mongoose), not a generic reminder — worth being explicit rather than assuming "we use Zod" automatically covers it (it does, if schemas are written strictly, but that's an implementation discipline point worth stating, not assuming).
**Recommended solution:** Add an explicit line to §16: all Zod schemas for query/body inputs must use strict primitive types (`z.string()`, not `z.any()`/loosely-typed objects) specifically to prevent MongoDB operator injection, and add a lint/review rule enforcing it.
**Priority:** **Medium**

### S4. CSRF defense-in-depth and cross-subdomain cookie scoping not addressed
**Raised by:** Security Architect
**Issue:** `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §9.1 relies on `SameSite=Strict` for the refresh-token cookie. It doesn't address (a) whether `SameSite=Strict` alone is considered sufficient without a secondary CSRF token, or (b) how cookie domain scoping works given `storefront` and `admin` are separate Next.js apps (§4) potentially on different subdomains.
**Why it is a problem:** `SameSite=Strict` is strong but not universally sufficient defense-in-depth for high-value actions (e.g., admin refund actions), and subdomain cookie scoping is a common source of real bugs (either the cookie doesn't work across the intended subdomain, or it's scoped more broadly than intended).
**Recommended solution:** Explicitly state the cookie domain strategy for `storefront` vs `admin` (separate cookies scoped per-app is safer than a shared parent-domain cookie), and add a CSRF token requirement for state-changing admin actions as defense-in-depth beyond `SameSite`.
**Priority:** **Medium**

### S5. Trusted-upload path doesn't specify Cloudinary upload-preset constraints
**Raised by:** Security Architect
**Issue:** `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §15 correctly separates trusted (admin/catalog) direct-to-Cloudinary uploads from untrusted user-generated uploads, but never states that the trusted path's signed upload parameters are still scoped by a Cloudinary upload preset restricting file type and size.
**Why it is a problem:** A compromised admin/staff credential could otherwise upload arbitrarily large or arbitrary-type files directly to Cloudinary — the backend never sees the binary (by design, §15.1), so the *only* enforcement point available is the signed upload preset's own constraints, and the design doesn't confirm those constraints exist.
**Recommended solution:** State explicitly that signed upload params are generated against a restricted Cloudinary upload preset (`allowed_formats`, `max_file_size`), not an unrestricted one, even for the trusted path.
**Priority:** **Medium**

### S6. No secrets-rotation cadence defined
**Raised by:** Security Architect, DevOps Architect
**Issue:** `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §16 states secrets are environment-injected and never committed (good baseline) but defines no rotation cadence for JWT signing secrets, Razorpay keys, Cloudinary API secret, or DB credentials.
**Why it is a problem:** "Never committed" addresses one risk class; rotation addresses a different one (credential compromise blast-radius over time) — standard production hygiene, currently unaddressed.
**Recommended solution:** Define a rotation cadence (e.g., quarterly for API keys, immediate on suspected compromise for all) and confirm the secret-store tooling supports rotation without a full redeploy.
**Priority:** **Low**

## 7. Findings — Performance

### P1. Dual-cache invalidation gap: Redis API cache vs. Next.js ISR/edge cache
**Raised by:** Staff Engineer, Solution Architect
**Issue:** `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §12 (Product Flow) describes Redis cache-aside invalidation on product update. §2 mentions "SSR/ISR storefront" for the Next.js app. These are two independent caching layers, and nothing in either document states how a product/price update propagates through *both* — the Redis invalidation step never triggers an ISR on-demand revalidation of the corresponding Next.js page.
**Why it is a problem:** Without this, a customer could see a stale price rendered from the ISR-cached page even after the API-layer cache has been correctly invalidated — and then be charged a different price at checkout (since checkout re-validates against live data per the Order Flow's own correctness rules, §11). That mismatch is a real trust and potentially legal/consumer-protection issue for an eCommerce checkout, not just a caching nicety.
**Recommended solution:** Add an explicit step to the Product Flow: on product/price update, trigger both the Redis cache invalidation *and* a Next.js on-demand revalidation call (`revalidatePath`/`revalidateTag`) for affected storefront pages.
**Priority:** **High**

### P2. Faceted-filter aggregation runs uncached on every request
**Raised by:** Database Architect, Staff Engineer
**Issue:** `03_database_design.md` §11.5 describes a `$facet` pipeline for catalog filter counts, run per category-page request. `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §12's Product Flow only caches the product *list*, not the facet-count sidebar.
**Why it is a problem:** `$facet` pipelines are CPU-intensive and don't get the same indexed-scan efficiency across all sub-pipelines simultaneously — running this fresh on every category-page load is a specific, identifiable database bottleneck at moderate catalog-browsing traffic.
**Recommended solution:** Cache facet-count results in Redis with the same category/filter-context key and a short TTL (facet counts don't need to be real-time-accurate to the second).
**Priority:** **Medium**

### P3. `$unwind`-before-`$lookup` ordering in the Sales Revenue aggregation is inefficient
**Raised by:** Database Architect
**Issue:** `03_database_design.md` §11.2 unwinds `orders.items` before looking up `products` for category — at scale this is less efficient than reducing cardinality first (e.g., grouping by category-relevant keys before the join, or restructuring the lookup).
**Why it is a problem:** A specific, fixable inefficiency in a report that will be run frequently (admin revenue dashboard).
**Recommended solution:** Reorder or restructure the pipeline; benchmark both orderings against realistic order volume before finalizing.
**Priority:** **Low**

### P4. Mongo text-index search is a UX risk, not only a "future scale" concern
**Raised by:** Staff Engineer, Solution Architect
**Issue:** `03_database_design.md` §10.4 correctly frames the Mongo text index as "the correct MVP-stage baseline," but neither doc mentions that Mongo text search has no typo-tolerance and weak relevance-tuning compared to what a Pepperfry/IKEA-caliber catalog search experience requires.
**Why it is a problem:** This was framed purely as a scale trade-off ("fine until traffic grows") when it's also a day-one UX-quality trade-off against the named competitive benchmarks in `01_business_research.md`.
**Recommended solution:** No architecture change needed — just reframe the trade-off explicitly in `03_database_design.md` §10.4 as UX-relevant from day one, so the MVP team makes an informed call rather than assuming "search is a Phase 2 problem."
**Priority:** **Medium**

## 8. Findings — Production Readiness

### PR1. No degraded-mode behavior defined for third-party outages
**Raised by:** DevOps Architect
**Issue:** The platform's core flows depend on three external providers (Razorpay, Cloudinary, WhatsApp Business API). No document defines what the system does when one of them is down — e.g., can checkout fall back to COD-only if Razorpay is unreachable? Are image uploads queued locally if Cloudinary is down?
**Why it is a problem:** Without a defined degraded mode, the default behavior during a third-party outage is "the feature is simply broken," which is avoidable for at least the highest-value flows (checkout, lead capture).
**Recommended solution:** Define degraded-mode behavior for checkout (COD fallback) and lead capture (accept the lead even if WhatsApp confirmation fails, retry via queue) explicitly in `02_enterprise_architecture.md`.
**Priority:** **Medium**

### PR2. No RPO/RTO targets defined for backup/restore
**Raised by:** DevOps Architect, Database Architect
**Issue:** `03_database_design.md` §14–§15 and `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §17 discuss backup posture generally ("automated backups and tested restore procedure") but define no concrete Recovery Point Objective or Recovery Time Objective.
**Why it is a problem:** "We have backups" isn't a disaster-recovery plan without a target for how much data loss and downtime is acceptable — those targets drive the actual backup frequency and restore-testing cadence decisions.
**Recommended solution:** Define explicit RPO/RTO targets (e.g., RPO ≤ 1 hour, RTO ≤ 4 hours for MVP) and size the backup/replication strategy to meet them.
**Priority:** **Medium**

### PR3. No fail-fast configuration validation at process boot
**Raised by:** DevOps Architect
**Issue:** Neither document mentions validating required environment variables/secrets at container startup before accepting traffic.
**Why it is a problem:** Without this, a missing/misconfigured secret surfaces as a runtime error on the first request that needs it (e.g., the first payment) rather than as an immediate, loud startup failure — worse failure mode, harder to catch in CI/staging.
**Recommended solution:** Add explicit boot-time config validation (fail the container immediately, before passing health checks, if required config is missing) to the production-readiness checklist.
**Priority:** **Low**

### PR4. Rollback trigger criteria undefined
**Raised by:** DevOps Architect
**Issue:** `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §17 lists "Rollback strategy defined" as a checklist item without stating what triggers a rollback (error-rate threshold, manual-only, latency SLO breach).
**Why it is a problem:** A rollback "strategy" that's purely manual-judgment-based is real but weaker than one gated on defined metrics — worth deciding deliberately rather than defaulting to "someone notices and rolls back."
**Recommended solution:** Define at least one automated rollback trigger (e.g., error-rate threshold on the health-check-gated deploy) before this checklist item is considered complete.
**Priority:** **Low**

## 9. Findings — Developer Experience

### DX1. No Git branching/PR review policy defined
**Raised by:** Engineering Manager
**Issue:** None of the four documents specify a branching strategy (trunk-based vs. Gitflow), required PR approvals, or merge-gating rules, despite `04_architecture_decision.md` §10.1 already listing `CODEOWNERS` as a root-level file.
**Why it is a problem:** `CODEOWNERS` without a stated review-requirement policy is an unused file — team-collaboration mechanics need to be decided before a team starts committing, not discovered ad hoc.
**Recommended solution:** Define branching strategy and minimum-approval PR policy (likely trunk-based with 1 required review + passing CI, given team size) as part of the Phase 5 kickoff, not left implicit.
**Priority:** **Medium**

### DX2. No process to keep the four architecture documents in sync with the implementation
**Raised by:** Engineering Manager, Principal Architect
**Issue:** `01`–`04` cross-reference each other extensively by section number. `04_architecture_decision.md` §10.7 plans future `docs/adr/` additions but no document establishes that architecturally significant code changes must update the relevant doc section as part of the same PR.
**Why it is a problem:** Documentation this detailed and this cross-linked has real value only as long as it stays accurate — without an update discipline, it will drift from the real system within a few sprints and become actively misleading rather than merely stale.
**Recommended solution:** Add a lightweight rule (e.g., a PR template checkbox: "Does this change require a docs/ update?") and assign doc ownership per module, consistent with the CODEOWNERS approach already planned.
**Priority:** **Medium**

### DX3. No CI test-coverage threshold defined
**Raised by:** Staff Engineer
**Issue:** `02_enterprise_architecture.md` §16–§17 mention unit and integration tests as CI gates but define no minimum coverage threshold.
**Why it is a problem:** "Tests exist" and "tests exist at a meaningful coverage level" are different bars; without a stated threshold, coverage tends to erode under deadline pressure.
**Recommended solution:** Define a minimum coverage threshold (commonly 70-80% for Application/Domain layers specifically, where the business logic lives, rather than a blanket repo-wide number that over-weights generated/boilerplate code) as a CI gate.
**Priority:** **Low**

## 12. Positive Findings — What the Board Explicitly Endorses

A review this critical is only credible if it's also honest about what's genuinely good — otherwise it reads as reflexive fault-finding rather than independent judgment. The board wants the following on record as decisions to *keep*, not to revisit:

- **`01_business_research.md`'s own gap analysis** — the source document (before any of these four docs existed) correctly identified that the original feature checklist inverted the stated business priorities, and the phased MVP roadmap that resulted is well-sequenced against Design → Leads → Commerce rather than the checklist's page order. This is the right instinct applied consistently.
- **Razorpay webhook as the sole source of truth for payment state**, never the client-side callback (`02_enterprise_architecture.md` §11) — this is the correct pattern and it's applied consistently to both furniture orders and design-project milestones via the polymorphic `payments` collection, rather than reimplementing payment logic twice.
- **The embed-vs-reference reasoning throughout `03_database_design.md`** is genuinely sound, not just asserted — variants embedded (bounded, co-read, same lifecycle) vs. inventory referenced (fast-changing, needs per-warehouse breakdown) is exactly the right call, and it's applied with the same rigor to `orders.items` snapshotting for immutability and `design_project_assets` referencing for unbounded media growth.
- **The Interior Design state machine modeled as an explicit, auditable state transition** (`02_enterprise_architecture.md` §13) rather than a flat status field — this decision alone is what makes the funnel analytics and dispute-resolution needs identified in doc01 actually achievable later.
- **The framing correction in `04_architecture_decision.md` §1** — correctly identifying that Monorepo/Polyrepo is an orthogonal axis to runtime architecture rather than forcing it into the same five-way comparison the prompt implied — is exactly the kind of pushback a real architecture review wants to see, not agreement for its own sake.
- **The extraction-readiness checklist and ranked candidate list in `04_architecture_decision.md` §9** — refusing to extract Analytics, Search, or Notifications until a concrete trigger exists, while still designing the module boundaries so extraction is mechanical when the time comes, is textbook "build the on-ramp, don't take the exit yet."
- **Every document's willingness to say "confirm with the business" rather than guessing** (the Open Items sections in all four documents) — an architecture that fabricates certainty about unanswered business questions (inventory ownership model, designer employment model, physical studios) would be a worse document than one that flags them honestly.

None of the findings in Sections 3–9 contradict these — they're gaps and omissions layered on top of a sound foundation, not evidence the foundation is wrong.

## 13. Final Verdict

**Would this be approved for production by senior engineers at Shopify or Amazon? No — not yet.**

Not because the architecture is wrong. The modular-monolith-plus-monorepo decision, the Clean Architecture layering, the embed-vs-reference database discipline, and the phased MVP-to-Hybrid roadmap would all likely survive a Shopify/Amazon review largely intact — that reasoning is sound and well-justified against the actual business priorities, not generic boilerplate. The verdict is No because four Critical and nine High-priority items (Section 10) represent exactly the class of gap those reviews are designed to catch before code gets written: a core workflow that can't be staffed correctly (B1), a compliance exposure (B4), a data-integrity race condition (D1), and a baseline security control missing from privileged accounts (S1). Any one of these shipping to production as-is would be a legitimate incident or audit finding, not a style nitpick.

This is a gate, not a rejection. The path to approval is Section 13, not a restart.
